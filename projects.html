APSC 200

Volleyball Knee Pad

As a group, we were tasked with designing a volleyball knee pad with an internal rubber microstructure to reduce knee injuries caused by impact forces and rapid changes in direction during gameplay. Objectives also included an effective design for female bodies and the ability to dissipate 15 percent of 2,000-newton impact forces. Tests included shear, lateral tension and compression, and vertical compression under 2,000-newton and 4,000-newton forces. The final lattice-structure implements diagonal beams on each face of the structure to protect the product from damage caused by shear forces. The predicted porosity was 50 percent to improve breathability.

QUEEN'S NEXT GENERATION HACKATHON

Neuroneck

Placed 1st in the Queen's Next Generation of Medical Simulation Hackathon, hosted by Ingenuity Labs. In a fast-paced team environment, developed a solution to help medical professionals better train to treat spinal injuries. The final prototype was a neck brace that offered users feedback when treating spinal injuries through a set of pressure sensors and LED lights. Our team leaned on our diverse set of skills and experiences to develop a new and innovative product that explored an area of medicine not yet addressed.

APSC 101

Robotic Arm

The project tasked the team with designing and prototyping an assistive arm to aid people with mobility limitations, such as those caused by arthritis, in completing everyday tasks. The robot successfully completed all challenges and exceeded expectations by lifting a 25 g object to a height of 20 cm and moving it 20 cm in the plane during testing.

APSC 103

Balloon Gondola

The Queen's Space Engineering Team requested the design and prototyping of a high-altitude gondola capable of housing ToastSats and enduring environmental conditions and crash landings. Testing and design iterations resulted in a final two-part box design made from extruded polystyrene panels, with 3D-printed internal supports and heat-shield wrapping.
